01 · The Decoupling

Venture funding and SME hiring are moving in different directions.

Through the exact window in which Southeast Asia’s venture deal count sank to its lowest level in nearly a decade, SME hiring activity on Glints more than quadrupled. Small employers do not hire because sentiment improved. They hire because orders came in.

02 · The New Employer Class

Half of the SMEs hiring today have never hired on a platform before.

In Jan–Sep 2026, 59% of small employers posting on Glints were doing so for the first time ever — businesses entering the formal hiring market, many making their first hire, full stop. And the fastest-growing employer class is the smallest: companies with fewer than ten people are now nearly one in three hiring SMEs, up from one in five two years ago.

03 · What SMEs Hire Now

The boom is the real economy — plus a creator class that barely existed three years ago.

The fastest-rising SME roles are not what the AI-doom discourse predicts. They are drivers, bakers, production workers, sales promoters — the jobs of physical commerce growing again. Alongside them, a creator-economy hiring cluster grew 53% year on year: live-commerce hosts, social media managers, e-commerce operators. Content Creator is now a top-8 SME role outright.

    04 · Speed Is the Product

    An SME job posted at lunch has applicants before the coffee is cold.

    SME hiring runs on a different clock from enterprise recruitment. No requisition cycles, no two-week job-ad campaigns. The median SME job on Glints receives its first application in about 12 minutes, and 94.6% receive one within 24 hours. On the employer side, half of all shortlisting decisions happen within a day of the application arriving.

    A Letter from Glints

    Why we started counting

    Read LinkedIn or the tech press right now and Southeast Asia looks like it is shrinking. Funding charts point down. Layoff trackers keep filling up. Every second post is about AI coming for white-collar jobs. Spend a day there and you would decide the region’s best years are behind it.

    We have been building in a different part of the region since 2013. It looks nothing like that.

    Last month we met a woman who runs 3 coffee shops in Jakarta. She needed a barista by Saturday. Her third outlet was opening that day, staffed or not. She was hiring with Instagram stories, a WhatsApp group and hope. The drop in venture funding never reached her. Venture money never had. She hired because she opened a third shop.

    She is the market. The vast majority of Southeast Asia’s enterprises are small businesses, and they employ most of its workforce. Almost none of the hiring tools built in the last 20 years were built for them.

    What the data says

    This is the first edition of the index. We started counting properly this year, and 3 things surprised us.

    First, SME job postings have more than tripled since January 2024. That happened in the same months that venture deal count in the region fell to its lowest level in nearly 10 years. Funding fell. Hiring rose.

    Second, about 1 in 2 small employers hiring today had never hired on a platform before. Tens of thousands of businesses each half-year post a job for the first time. The fastest-growing group of employers is also the smallest: companies with fewer than 10 people.

    Third, the roles. This surprised us most. The fastest-rising jobs are drivers, bakers, production workers and field sales. Next to them are jobs that barely existed 3 years ago, like live-commerce hosts, content creators and social media operators. Those jobs are growing 50% a year. The wages now compete with accounting.

    Why job platforms missed this

    Job platforms grew up serving big companies, because that is where the budgets were. Annual contracts. Ad packages priced in the thousands. Employer branding suites. All of that makes sense for a bank hiring 500 people a year.

    A coffee chain with 3 outlets needs 5 good candidates in a chat thread by tomorrow. The price has to be close to 1 day of revenue from 1 store. Ask the owner to post an ad and wait 2 weeks, and you have already lost her. Serving her means pricing for a single hire, working inside chat, and matching that does the shortlisting she has no time to do. We spent 10 years learning how to do that, mostly the hard way. We got a lot of it wrong the first time. Those 10 years are why this data exists at all.

    What AI is doing to small businesses

    The common view is that AI destroys jobs. Inside large companies, some of that is clearly happening. At the small end we see something else. AI makes it possible to run a much smaller company. A 5-person business that would once have closed now survives, grows, and hires a 6th, 7th and 8th person. Often for roles nobody had a name for 2 years ago. AI also cuts the cost of serving that business. Screening, shortlisting and working out what a role should pay used to need an expensive person. Now they happen in seconds inside a chat thread. Whoever serves these businesses well gets to bring millions of them into formal hiring for the first time.

    Where we could be wrong

    The drop in venture funding is real. AI disruption of white-collar work is real too. We think it gets worse inside large organisations before it gets better. An index built on 1 platform’s data has limits, which is why we published the methodology and what we left out. We are optimistic about a part of the economy we can measure. Whether everything else recovers is a different question, and probably not ours to answer. Whether this growth is structural or a lag is what the next editions of this index are for.

    The SEA economy is more than just the venture funding charts and there continues to be growth in different areas. Builders continue to build.

    The barista, by the way, started on Monday.
